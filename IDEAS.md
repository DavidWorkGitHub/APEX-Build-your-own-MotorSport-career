# Swami Formula — what's next

Four things you've already called, then a menu to pick the rest from.

---

## Confirmed

### 1. Team-mates who stay
Right now you get a new team-mate every season even at the same team, which quietly undercuts the head-to-head being the stat that matters. Instead:

- A team-mate has a **contract of their own** — one to four seasons.
- Long-term partners develop a **relationship state**: cordial, tense, or open war, moved by results, by decisions, and by who the team favours.
- Short-term ones are rotating benchmarks: a rookie brought in to be beaten, a veteran on a one-year deal.
- The head-to-head runs **across seasons**, not just within one, so "three years, 41–22" becomes a real line in the cabinet.
- When they leave, they go **somewhere** — and can turn up later as a rival, a team boss, or the driver who took the seat you wanted.

### 2. Ageing, decline, and the comeback
- Attributes **peak around 27–31** and fall after, and they don't fall evenly: reflexes (qualifying, racecraft) go first, judgement (tyres, feedback, consistency) holds much longer. A 36-year-old is slower on Saturday and cannier on Sunday.
- Decline is **visible on the driver card** — the ring gauge falls, and you feel it.
- **Retirement stops being a wall.** If you leave with credit in the bank — titles, reputation, no burnt bridges — a seat can open up one to three seasons later. An injury to someone else, a mid-season sacking, a team that needs a safe pair of hands.
- The comeback is **gated by how you left**. Mercenary and fragile reputations don't get the call. Loyal and political ones do.
- A comeback career is short and pointed: two or three seasons, worse machinery, and a completely different set of decisions about what you're doing there.

### 3. More scenarios
Ten scenes is enough to prove the idea and nowhere near enough for a twenty-season career. Target thirty to forty, in bands:

- **Junior** — money, family, school, a rival's father buying a seat.
- **F1 rookie** — a team order in your third race, a media pile-on, the first time an engineer lies to you.
- **Established** — a title fight, a team-mate veto in your contract, a rule change that kills your driving style.
- **Late career** — a young team-mate who's faster, a move to a worse team for a lead role, when to stop.
- **Repeatable** with different framing, so a long career never runs dry.

### 4. Past runs
The hall of fame is a list. This makes it a **place you can go back into**:

- Every retired driver keeps their **full season-by-season table**, their cabinet, and their decision log.
- Open an old career and browse it exactly as you did when it was live.
- **Compare two careers** side by side — same trait, different choices, different endings.
- A running **profile across all careers**: total titles, best driver, favourite trait, most-used decision.
- Export a career as a text card you can paste anywhere.

## Real drivers and teams

Worth being straight about this. Two options, and they're very different:

**Fictional grid, real feel (what's there now).** Generated names, generated teams. Nothing to worry about, and the grid changes every career, which suits a game about *your* driver rather than a specific era.

**Real names.** Fine for something you play yourself. Riskier if you ever put it online — F1 games are licensed for a reason, and that covers driver likenesses and team marks. The practical middle ground: build a **roster file** — one editable list of drivers and teams the game reads at startup. Ship it with fictional names, and anyone who wants a real grid can fill it in themselves. That also solves the staleness problem, since grids change every year and a hard-coded 2026 list is wrong by 2027.

If you want, I can also add an **era setting** — a roster per decade, so a career can start on a grid that feels like the 90s or the 2000s.

## Menu — pick a fifth (or more)

**Money.** Salary, a bank balance, and seats you can buy into. Makes the funding clause bite and gives the family-run team a reason to exist.

**Mid-season events.** Sackings, mid-year driver swaps, a safety car that ruins a win, a penalty that costs a podium, an upgrade that arrives and doesn't work.

**Title fights.** A named championship rival at your level, a points gap tracked round by round, and a final-round decider that's a decision, not a simulation.

**Full standings and race detail.** The whole championship table, and a per-race view: grid, finish, gap, who you passed.

**Resume a career.** It already saves after every race and never loads it back. Small job, real payoff.

**Press and public.** A one-line media reaction each season that reflects your reputation, and a fan rating that moves your options.

**Injuries and reliability arcs.** A team whose cars break for a whole season, an injury that costs you rounds and changes your attributes.

**Sound.** One engine note on season start and nothing else.

**Difficulty / seeds.** Enter a seed to replay the same career, or share one.

**Team boss mode.** After retirement, run a team. A whole second game, and the natural sequel to a career ending.

## My ranking, if it helps

1. **More scenarios** — the decisions *are* the game, and they run out.
2. **Team-mates who stay** — makes the most important stat mean something.
3. **Ageing and the comeback** — gives a career a shape instead of a plateau.
4. **Past runs** — the reason to play a second time.
5. **Title fights** — fixes the flat, tension-free top of the ladder.

Money and mid-season events are the strongest of the rest, and resume-a-career is the cheapest win on the whole list.
